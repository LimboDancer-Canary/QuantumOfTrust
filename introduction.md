# Introduction to Quantum of Trust

## Overview

Quantum of Trust (q<T>) is a revolutionary framework that decouples trust from identity, enabling both humans and AI agents to build reputation through verified actions rather than disclosure of personal information.

## The Challenge

In the emerging agent economy, traditional identity-based trust systems fall short:

- **Privacy vs. Trust**: Current systems require identity disclosure to establish trust
- **Agent Integration**: AI agents lack traditional identity markers
- **Reputation Portability**: Trust signals are often siloed within platforms
- **Action Verification**: Difficulty in verifying claims without revealing sensitive data

## The Solution

Quantum of Trust provides cryptographic primitives that enable:

1. **Action-Based Trust**: Reputation built through verified actions, not identity
2. **Universal Primitives**: Same trust mechanisms for humans and AI agents
3. **Privacy Preservation**: Zero-knowledge proofs enable verification without disclosure
4. **Composable Reputation**: Trust signals that work across platforms and contexts

## Key Principles

### Decoupling Trust from Identity

q<T> separates the concept of trust from personal identity. Participants can build reputation and demonstrate trustworthiness through provable actions without revealing who they are.

### Verified Action

Every trust signal is backed by cryptographic proof of action. This ensures that reputation is earned through actual behavior, not claimed credentials.

### Universal Application

The same primitives work for:
- Human participants
- AI agents
- Organizations
- Smart contracts
- Any entity that can perform verifiable actions

## Use Cases

- **Autonomous Agents**: AI agents building trust through task completion
- **Decentralized Markets**: Trade without identity disclosure
- **Privacy-Preserving Services**: Access based on reputation, not identity
- **Cross-Platform Reputation**: Portable trust signals across ecosystems

## Architecture

The Quantum of Trust framework consists of:

1. **Trust Primitives**: Core cryptographic building blocks
2. **Verification Layer**: Zero-knowledge proof systems
3. **Reputation Aggregation**: Composable trust metrics
4. **Platform Integration**: APIs for diverse ecosystems
